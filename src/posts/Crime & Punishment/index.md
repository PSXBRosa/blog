Back in January, as a resolution for the year of 2026, I set the goal of reading one great novel every month. And now, by the end of may (six months, for those who can't count.), I have the pleasure to announce for all who wish to hear that I have most efficiently finished my first book of the year. (I actually read a couple of silly YA novels during these past few months, but they barely merit these parenthesis.)

 !    
!     

I didn't know much about Dostoyevsky when I decided to pick this book up from my parents' bookshelf. I had bought the novel at a book fair almost nine years ago in a pile of impulse buys, most of which I never tried to read or were too boring to finish. When I finally grabbed Crime and Punishment earlier this year, there was no particular ambition to the choice. I had heard of its name before and it had been sitting waiting for me for almost a decade.
[//]: # put the picture of my editora 34 book

   
   

I'd love to tell you that it was an instant page turner, but you already know that it took me six months to finish. I could try to shift the blame to my environment, claiming an international move and eventually losing the book at the airport were the culprits. But a fellow reader of Russian literature would know why I have to be confess the truth. The real reason is that I, like many others, am struggling to read novels. Short-form video is a incredibly addicting, and I can barely operate outside of my wageslave routine. But! much like in the book, I hope to have found a quiet form of salvation at the end.
